TL;DR

Attackers drained Kelp DAO's rsETH bridge, deposited the stolen tokens on Aave V3 as collateral, and borrowed real wrapped ETH against them. Aave's code worked exactly as designed — the collateral it accepted was backed by an asset on a chain Aave doesn't control. When that backing vanished, the protocol was left with ~$196M in bad debt and ~$10B in panic withdrawals.

ATTACK MECHANICS

How $292M became Aave's problem

The exploit did not break cryptography or bypass Aave's smart contracts. It exploited a configuration weakness in cross-chain message verification, then used Aave's normal lending rules against it.

Forged cross-chain message on Kelp's bridge

Kelp DAO's bridge — built on LayerZero — accepts verified messages from other chains to release rsETH on Ethereum. Attackers submitted forged messages that passed the bridge's (misconfigured) verification layer.

116,500 rsETH released to attacker wallet

Roughly $292M worth of rsETH — ~18% of total rsETH supply — appears in an attacker address with no corresponding ETH backing on the source chain.

Unbacked rsETH deposited on Aave V3

Aave's oracle still reported rsETH at peg. The protocol saw valid-looking collateral and accepted it. Attackers deposited the full amount.

Wrapped ETH borrowed against fake collateral

Using the standard loan-to-value rules, attackers borrowed real WETH out of Aave's liquidity pools — across Aave, Compound and Euler, total borrowed reached ~$236M, with the bulk (~$196M) on Aave.

Aave freezes rsETH markets — too late for this batch

Borrowing stops on V3/V4 rsETH markets. But the existing bad-debt position is already open. Aave is left holding a claim against collateral whose backing no longer exists.

Whale-led bank run locks the pools

Large depositors (Justin Sun, MEXC, funds) race to withdraw. ETH, then USDT, then USDC pools hit 100% utilization. Remaining users can't withdraw — many start borrowing against their own stuck stables at ~75% LTV just to get any liquidity out.

ATTRIBUTION & CONTEXT

Attackers

Cyvers and LayerZero both signal likely North Korean affiliation, based on scale, tradecraft, and use of Tornado Cash for funding the origin wallet. This would place the incident alongside the April 1 Drift exploit (~$285M, also DPRK-attributed) in a pattern of large-scale DeFi targeting this month.

Cluster context

April 2026 has seen CoW Swap, Zerion, Rhea Finance, Silo Finance and Drift all exploited, on top of Kelp/Aave. The shared thread is cross-chain messaging and modular infrastructure with inconsistent security baselines — individually audited protocols inheriting risk from their integrations.

Aave-specific weak spot

Aave parted ways with risk provider Chaos Labs on April 6 over budget and direction of Aave V4. The Kelp incident lands two weeks later and represents the first real stress test of the Umbrella safety module introduced in June 2025. How the DAO resolves the deficit — Umbrella, stkAAVE slashing, treasury, or socialized — sets precedent.

PERSONAL PLAYBOOK · EXIT / HEDGE / MONITOR

What to actually do

These are decision prompts, not instructions. Your action depends on where your money actually sits. Start by locating yourself in the risk ranking on tab 02.

EXIT

// REDUCE_EXPOSURE IF YOU HOLD AT-RISK POSITIONS
  • Stuck in a frozen Aave pool? Don't panic-borrow blindly. The "borrow against your own stables at 75% LTV" escape hatch works but costs you 25% plus interest. Calculate whether you actually need liquidity now or can wait for utilization to normalize as loans get repaid.
  • If you hold rsETH — understand what you have. Aave says on-mainnet rsETH remains fully backed. Kelp has paused contracts. Exit liquidity is thin. Decide: hold and trust backing, or accept the market discount to get out cleanly when markets reopen.
  • Audit your other liquid restaking token exposure. ezETH, weETH, pufETH, other LRTs inherit the same class of bridge/cross-chain-backing risk. The fact that a specific bridge got exploited today doesn't mean others are safe — it means you now know the failure mode exists.
  • Consider reducing total Aave footprint if you're overweight. Not because the protocol is broken (it isn't), but because the Umbrella mechanism may need to absorb losses and governance direction is uncertain post-Chaos Labs split. Diversification across lenders is cheap insurance.
  • Move earmarked dry powder off-exchange / off-protocol. Hardware wallet, custodied stables, or cold storage. Liquidity you need to be sure of should not be earning yield in a protocol that might freeze.

HEDGE

// NEUTRALIZE_TAIL_RISK IF YOU CAN'T OR WON'T EXIT
  • Short or buy puts on AAVE if you hold significant spot. AAVE down 16% already. Further downside if Umbrella is tapped hard, stkAAVE slashes, or TVL doesn't recover. Deribit / Aevo / Hyperliquid perps let you offset a concentrated position without selling it.
  • Pair-trade: short AAVE against a cleaner DeFi lender. If you believe Aave specifically takes the reputational hit but DeFi-lending as a category survives, shorting AAVE against a basket (MORPHO, MKR, etc.) isolates the Aave-specific risk.
  • Hedge ETH exposure if you expect further contagion. So far ETH is only −2%. If you think DeFi de-risking has further to run, a partial ETH put hedge covers the tail. If you don't, skip — hedging costs money and this may already be the bottom of the move.
  • Do NOT hedge by selling stuck collateral at distressed prices. Re-borrowing your way into lower LTV is a real, visible cost. Make sure the hedge premium is cheaper than the loss you'd take to free up that collateral.

MONITOR

// SIGNALS_TO_WATCH IF YOU'RE CHOOSING TO WAIT
  • Aave governance forum & Snapshot. The proposal on how to cover the deficit (Umbrella draw vs. stkAAVE slash vs. treasury vs. socialized loss) is the single most important signal. Language shift from "will cover" to "explore paths" suggests this is still being negotiated.
  • Aavescan pool utilization — ETH, USDT, USDC. When utilization drops below 100%, normal withdrawals resume. This is your "is my money unstuck?" dashboard.
  • stkAAVE slashing events on-chain. If Umbrella is triggered, the event is visible. If you stake AAVE and the event fires, your position takes a real haircut — know before you read about it.
  • Kelp DAO status + rsETH reserve reports. Resumption of contracts, transparent post-mortem, and verified backing ratio are prerequisites for any normal rsETH price action. Silence or delay is bearish.
  • AAVE token at key levels. Current ~$90, down from ~$110. Watch whether it stabilizes, breaks down further, or recovers as Umbrella resolution becomes clearer. Volume matters more than price alone.
  • Other LRT/bridge protocols for "paused" announcements. SparkLend, Fluid, Lido earnETH, Ethena, Curve, BitGo WBTC have already paused LayerZero-related activity. More pauses = broader contagion. Resumptions = stabilization.
  • Attribution confirmation (DPRK/Lazarus). Regulatory response to confirmed North Korean attacks has historically been sharper than to generic exploits. Matters for downstream regulation of cross-chain infra.
DECISION FRAMING

The hardest part is separating three different questions: (1) Is Aave's protocol broken? — No. (2) Will Aave absorb this cleanly? — Unclear, depends on Umbrella resolution. (3) What's my exposure right now? — Only you can answer. Make decisions based on your actual position, not the headline.
